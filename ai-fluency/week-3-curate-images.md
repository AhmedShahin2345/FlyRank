## What I rejected, and why

**Rejected: AI-generated "confetti energy" hero texture.** First idea was a
generated confetti/sparkle field to make the home page feel "Gen Z". Two
problems: every generated version read as stock imagery, and the texture
competed with the headline — exactly the upstaging the week's session warned
about. A low-contrast, grain-only noise tile in the kit's own teal does the
same job (texture without subject) and cannot be mistaken for filler. The
rejection is why the kit stays calm: energy lives in the real campaign photos,
not in the background.

**Rejected: AI illustration of "the student marketer"** for the About page.
Where the subject is a person, a generated face is a lie the reader will
eventually detect. A real portrait is the only honest option, so the slot is a
photo, not a prompt.

## Where real captures beat AI, stated plainly

Every proof image (stats, dashboards, event shots) is real. AI can generate a
plausible dashboard, but a plausible one is worth less than a real one — the
whole portfolio proves I *did* these numbers. Generated work appears nowhere
in the proof column; its only allowed job is connective tissue, and even that
was hand-built in the end.

## Two-line rejection summary

Real captures carry every claim the portfolio makes; generated images only
risked competing with them. I kept one hand-built texture, and rejected every
generated option that could be mistaken for stock or for fake proof.
